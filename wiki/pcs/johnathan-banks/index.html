    Johnathan Banks¶

    Player: Ian · Archetype: Broker (homebrew) · Drive: "I just gotta ring the bell"

    Overview¶

    Wall Street didn't end when the world did — not for Johnathan Banks. He still talks in positions and upside, still reads a room like a spread, and still believes, somewhere under all of it, that the market is coming back and he will be standing there when the bell rings. He goes by JB, or Johnny B. Someone floated "Banksy" once; he didn't take to it. "I'm not into that art stuff. The only art I see is stocks going up and down."

    Before the outbreak he ran insider trades for mobsters. He rather hopes they're all dead. They probably aren't.

    The cabin the survivors now live in is his — the Banks family place, his father's or his grandfather's, at the end of a logging road nobody maintained even when there was somebody to maintain it. He was a rich, snarky punk with a hunting cabin he barely used, and he has spent the five years since taking in stragglers against what he insists is his better judgment. He is not warm about it. He keeps doing it anyway.

    He carries no weapon. He carries letters of introduction to three other havens, which he considers the better armament.

    In Character¶

    "I like the hockey stick. Up and to the right."

    "You're necessary for me to live."

    "Y'all are gonna hate me, but you'll need me."

    Drive — get back to Wall Street. "I just gotta ring the bell."

    Flaw — morally flexible, and cheerfully aware of it. He intends to be the kind of man the group wants to kill and can't afford to.

    Capabilities¶

    • Key skill: Manipulation. Empathy is his best attribute, and he spent his points on talking people into things.
    • Talent: Silver Tongue.
    • Gear: sorted small-value trade goods; a set of scales to weigh them; and letters of introduction to three other havens. He rolled the scales twice — "in case one of them's off, I like to check my work."
    • No weapon at all.

    Anchor¶

    Sister Ruth Okafor — the hospice nurse. He met her at the retirement home, one of the havens he holds a letter for, and talked her into leaving with him: "Just read these people their last rites and then come help us." He recruited her to the Hollow. He is entirely clear-eyed that this was an awful thing to do and did it anyway.

    Johnny Bank Notes¶

    A man walked out of the treeline at the crash site with five armed people behind him, and the first thing he did was use a name nobody at the Hollow had ever heard: Johnny Bank Notes. Wade Tolliver knew Banks from before. After the outbreak he offered Banks a place with the crew that now holds the Last Stop — fifty people, in the plaza Banks would later stand in front of and campaign to be king of — and Banks turned it down.

    Neither man has explained the refusal, and Banks did not volunteer it to the rest of the group.

    Relationships¶

    • Clara "CJ" James — his neighbor from before, another wealthy household on the same street. When it fell apart he took her with him. She is the reason the cabin is still standing.
    • Dr. Charles Greenbriar — arrived at the Hollow as a stranger, on foot, out of the woods, with Dale Pruitt in tow.
    • Ashley Fairfax — pure happenstance. She turned up. He let her stay.

    Session History¶

    • Nine in the Hollow — established as the owner of the Hollow and the reluctant reason anyone else is living in it. Took Sister Ruth as his Anchor. Confirmed the retirement home as one of his three letters of introduction; the other two are still open.
    • Smoke on the Ridge — spent the morning teaching Marcus Webb knife fighting, a subject he knows almost nothing about. Refused to let Marcus come to the crash and was overruled. Found the cockpit, took a dying pilot's last words, and then walked the boy in and had him do the hatchet work on her. Recognized, and named, by Wade Tolliver.
