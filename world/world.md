## The Outbreak & the Five Years

It's 2030. Five years since it started, and nobody agrees on what "it started"
really means — the first quarantine order, the last coherent broadcast, or the
day their own street went quiet. There was no cure and no rescue. The
governments spent their last good months assigning blame; the labs spent theirs
arguing over credit. By the time anyone agreed on what to do, no one was
organized enough to do it.

Everyone carries the virus. It doesn't matter how you die — walker bite, bullet,
starvation, sickness, old age — everyone comes back. A bite or a scratch always
kills, because the wound turns; the only hope is to take the limb off, and fast.

Nobody at the table has agreed on where it came from, and the survivors' own
theories are worse than useless. [Dr. Greenbriar](../docs/wiki/pcs/charles-greenbriar.md)
will tell you, at length, that it started when Bigfoot caught COVID. The
[Watchman](../docs/wiki/npcs/the-watchman.md) says it's the chemicals in the
water. There is no reason to believe either of them, and no one out here in a
position to prove otherwise.

## Dead Ridge & the Mountains

Every town, every hollow, every stretch of highway answers to whoever's strong
enough to hold it. Groups write their own laws now and enforce them however they
see fit. Some of those laws keep people alive; some get people killed faster than
the dead do.

The campaign sits in the southeastern corner of Tennessee where it runs into
Georgia — the ridges and river valleys north of **Chattanooga**. This is
Cumberland Plateau country: steep hillsides, hollows the maps never bothered to
name, logging roads that wash out every spring, and the river threading through
all of it.

Names spoken at the table: **Soddy-Daisy**, **Dunlap**, **Spring City**,
**Chattanooga** itself. The survivors settled on a stretch near Soddy-Daisy —
close enough to the water to drink and fish, close enough to the woods to hunt,
and far enough from the city that whatever else is living in Chattanooga hasn't
come looking yet.

What makes this place survivable is the same thing that makes it a trap. The
terrain is hard to cross, which keeps most people out — but the herds here
**climb**, and terrain has stopped being a wall.

### Soddy-Daisy & the River

The sector the group claimed. Plenty of access to water, plenty of natural
resources, and not quite in the city. There are islands out in the river,
which the survivors noted as an escape option if the ridge ever stops being
safe.

### The Old Yeller

The landmark everyone in the area gives directions by: a school bus, still
sitting where it stopped. See [The Old Yeller](../docs/wiki/locations/the-old-yeller.md).

## The Haven

**[The Hollow](../docs/wiki/locations/the-hollow.md)** — a hunting cabin at the
end of a washed-out logging road. **Capacity 1, Defense 2.**

Thick log walls, a single door, shuttered windows, and a wood stove. The forest
is the real wall: the road in is so overgrown that vehicles can't make the last
half-mile, so anything coming for you comes on foot, and you hear it first.

Before the outbreak it belonged to the **Banks family** — the father or
grandfather of [Johnathan Banks](../docs/wiki/pcs/johnathan-banks.md), who
inherited it and, against his own better judgment, has been taking in stragglers
ever since.

**Nine people live there:** the four survivors and the five who were already at
the cabin.

What it can't do:

- **One way in, one way out.** The logging road is easy to block or booby-trap —
  and just as easy for someone else to block first.
- **The root cellar's nearly bare.** Hunting and foraging aren't optional.
- **Woodsmoke gives you away.** The stove is the only heat through a mountain
  winter, and the smoke carries down into the valley.
- **The well runs low in dry months.** Fresh water isn't guaranteed past midsummer.

### The Other Havens

Two more places were on the map and passed over. They still exist out there.

- **The Last Stop** — an unfinished Buc-ee's off the old mountain highway, fifty
  pumps and a fifteen-foot fiberglass mascot visible for miles. Capacity 2,
  Defense 1. Too big to lock down, too loud to hide, and the propane cage out
  back is still full. The group wanted it and knew better.
- **The Ark** — a grounded two-story houseboat in a quiet cove, out of fuel and
  going nowhere. Capacity 1, Defense 2. The water is a moat nobody built on
  purpose; the rowboat is the only way off.

## Factions & Groups

Nothing organized has been named yet. What exists so far are places
[Banks](../docs/wiki/pcs/johnathan-banks.md) can get a door opened at — he
carries **letters of introduction to three other havens**.

- **The retirement home.** Confirmed as one of the three. The survivors there are
  said to use walkers as a barricade — literal walkers, the wheeled kind, and the
  other kind too. This is where Banks met [Sister Ruth](../docs/wiki/npcs/ruth-okafor.md)
  and talked her into leaving with him.
- **Two more** are undecided. Banks names one; the GM names the last.

## Threats

- **The climbing herd.** The defining local threat. These walkers come up the
  sides of the mountains — people have seen them in trees. Elevation is not
  cover here, and a ridge is not a wall.
- **Scarcity.** The Hollow's root cellar is nearly empty. Everything the nine of
  them eat has to be hunted, foraged, or taken.
- **Winter and woodsmoke.** They cannot survive the cold without the stove, and
  the stove announces them to the whole valley.
- **The dry season.** The well drops when the summer heat sets in.
- **Other people.** Nothing has come up the logging road yet. That is a matter of
  time, not of safety.

## Rumors & the World Beyond

People still talk about somewhere better — that the government held on out west,
that there's an America still standing behind walls and satellites and soldiers
who never stopped taking orders. Nobody anyone's met has been there. Nobody
anyone's met has met anyone who's been there.

Closer to home:

- **The Watchman is still broadcasting.** Not a loop — a live voice, turning up
  on the wrong frequency every so often with a new conspiracy. As long as the
  broadcasts keep changing, he's alive. See
  [the Watchman](../docs/wiki/npcs/the-watchman.md).
- **It's the chemicals in the water.** The Watchman's theory, and it has taken
  hold with exactly the sort of people you'd expect.
- **The Old Yeller is haunted.** Some say it. Nobody has offered to go check.
- **Something is still in there.** A walker was seen to *pause* before attacking a
  child. One report, no explanation, and it has bothered everyone who's heard it.

## Unsetting Answers

Session zero output. These are canon and outrank anything written above. Each
player rolled once on the Unsetting Questions table.

**1. Every group has a story about a herd that behaves wrong. What have you heard
about the one in this region?**

> **They climb.** People have seen them up in trees, coming up the sides of the
> mountains the way bears do — over the hillside before you know they're there.
> They scale the hills.

**2. What's the strangest thing anyone's ever seen a walker do that made them
wonder if something's still in there?**

> **One paused before attacking a child.** Just paused. Nobody has an explanation
> and nobody likes it.

**3. What's the story behind the wreck, ruin, or broken-down vehicle everyone in
the area uses as a landmark?**

> A **school bus** — **"the Old Yeller."** The driver had a heart attack behind
> the wheel and turned with a busload of kids aboard. Some people say the bus is
> haunted.

**4. What piece of radio chatter — a broadcast, a loop message, a voice on the
wrong frequency — do people out here still talk about?**

> A man who calls himself **the Watchman**, after the graphic novel, which he
> took very seriously. He spouts conspiracy theories on a frequency you find by
> tuning a little too far. It isn't a recording: the broadcasts are new each
> time, which means he's still out there. He may go silent.
