# The Hollow

A hunting cabin at the end of a washed-out logging road, tucked into a fold of the ridge above Soddy-Daisy — thick log walls, a single door, shuttered windows, and a wood stove that doesn't announce itself with electric light. The forest is the real wall here: the road in is so overgrown and washed out that vehicles can't make the last half-mile, so anything coming for you comes on foot, and you hear it first. Nine people live inside a cabin built for deer season.

**Capacity 1 · Defense 2** — the survivors' haven.

## Before

It was the **Banks family** cabin — Johnathan Banks' father's place, or his
grandfather's. Built for deer season, not for the end of the world. Banks was a
rich, snarky punk who barely used it, and it is the only thing he owned that
turned out to matter.

## Now

Nine people: the four survivors and the five who were already here —
[Birdie Coyle](../npcs/birdie-coyle.md), [Sister Ruth Okafor](../npcs/ruth-okafor.md),
[Tucker Vance](../npcs/tucker-vance.md), [Marcus Webb](../npcs/marcus-webb.md),
and [Dale Pruitt](../npcs/dale-pruitt.md). Capacity is rated for far fewer. People
sleep on floors and outside.

They chose the sector deliberately: near **Soddy-Daisy**, with river access and
natural resources close at hand, but not sitting on top of Chattanooga and
whatever is still living in it. There are islands out in the water, noted as an
escape route nobody has needed yet.

## What It Can't Do

- **One way in, one way out.** The logging road is easy to block or booby-trap —
  and just as easy for somebody else to block first. There is no good way to run.
- **The root cellar's nearly bare.** Whoever lived here before cleaned it out.
  Hunting and foraging aren't optional; they're survival.
- **Woodsmoke gives you away.** The stove is the only thing keeping nine people
  warm through a mountain winter, and its smoke can be seen and smelled from the
  valley below.
- **The well runs low in dry months.** Fresh water isn't guaranteed once the
  summer heat sets in.

And the local threat undoes the one advantage the terrain was supposed to give
them: the herds in these mountains **climb**. A cabin high on a ridge at the end
of an impassable road is only safe from things that need a road.
