# The Crash Site

A wooded slope five to ten miles out from [the Hollow](the-hollow.md), where a single-engine cargo plane clipped the ridgeline and came apart across roughly a hundred and fifty yards of timber. The fuselage is a torn open thing at the bottom of a furrow of shattered saplings, its cargo pod burst, faded purple and orange paint still readable under five years of grime. Debris is scattered up the slope and hung in the branches above it — paper, insulation, crates, a cooler, a seat cushion. Smoke still comes off the wing root, thin and grey against a low ceiling of cloud, and the whole hillside smells of aviation fuel strongly enough to taste. Anything with ears heard it come down.

**Not a haven** — a windfall with a countdown on it.

## What Was There

- **The aircraft** — an ex-FedEx Cessna Caravan running on hoarded avgas. It
  didn't fall out of the sky five years ago; it was flying *today*, which is the
  part nobody at the Hollow has stopped thinking about.
- **Six passengers plus cargo**, per the manifest fragment. Two crew in the
  cockpit. One of them was still alive when the party reached her.
- **The scattered salvage** — a satellite radio with a dead battery, a solar
  battery, a semi-automatic rifle with crash damage, an empty cooler, a case of
  money, and hospital-grade medical supplies.
- **A military-grade sample case**, stenciled **IVY-4**, which did not survive
  the impact. Vials broken, contents contaminated or destroyed.
- **Walkers that climb.** The mountains' own rule, demonstrated: one came out
  along a limb overhead and dropped. By the end of the afternoon a herd had
  followed the noise and the smell in from the surrounding trees.

## What It Means

The plane was fleeing something. It came out of a place these mountains only know
as a fireside rumor — *West* — refueled once at a relay strip close enough to be a
real destination, and was headed east to somewhere called
[Station Ivy](station-ivy.md). Every one of those is a place with fuel, a radio,
and people organized enough to expect an aircraft by a particular hour.

The dying pilot's instruction was not *get help*. It was **don't radio — they're
listening**.
