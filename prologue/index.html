    Prologue¶

    The Walking Dead Universe Roleplaying Game — Tennessee/Georgia mountains, 2030.

    It's 2030. Five years since it started, though nobody agrees on what "it started" really means.

    Some people mark it from the first quarantine order. Some mark it from the last coherent news broadcast. Some mark it from the day their own street went quiet. Truth is, by the time anyone had a name for what was happening, it had already happened. Nobody saw it coming, because everybody was looking somewhere else — another war on another continent, another market crash, another feed of cat videos to scroll past at two in the morning. The world had gotten very good at watching a hundred disasters at once and taking none of them seriously. This one was different.

    What should scare you more than the dead is how fast everything else fell. The nations that called themselves superpowers spent their last good months arguing about whose fault it was, instead of doing anything about it. Generals fought with politicians. Politicians fought with each other. By the time anyone agreed on what to do, there was no one left organized enough to do it. The scientists didn't do much better. All that knowledge, all those labs, all that certainty that a problem like this gets solved — and it turned out knowing what something is isn't the same as stopping it. The people who might have found an answer together spent too long fighting over who'd get the credit if they did, and who'd take the blame if they didn't. In the end, it didn't matter. There was no cure. There was no rescue. There was only what came next.

    What came next was simple, and it hasn't gotten more complicated since. Every town, every hollow, every stretch of highway answers to whoever's strong enough to hold it. Groups write their own laws now, and enforce them however they see fit. Some of those laws keep people alive. Some of them get people killed just as fast as the dead do. You learn quickly which is which, or you don't survive long enough to learn anything at all.

    People still talk about somewhere better — some say the government held on out west, that there's an America still standing behind walls and satellites and soldiers who never stopped taking orders. Nobody you've ever met has been there. Nobody you've ever met has met anyone who's been there. It's the kind of story people tell each other on bad nights, the same way they used to tell ghost stories — not because it's true, but because believing it costs nothing and might get you through till morning.

    What's real is closer than that, and a lot less comforting. It's the sound a herd makes before you can see it. It's the quiet that comes right before a stranger's hand goes for a weapon instead of a handshake. Out here, in these mountains, the dead are the least complicated thing you'll face. The next encounter — whatever's on the other end of it — might be the last one you get.

    Welcome to Tennessee. Welcome to Georgia. Welcome to what's left.

    Where this leaves you

    Five years in. No cure, no rescue, no authority left worth the name. What happens next in these mountains hasn't been decided yet — that's session zero. Bring a character who has already survived something.
